### The Roland JP-08

Now that the Jupiter-08 synthesizer by Roland has come out, I should
compare it to its spiritual ancester, the [JP-08](https://www.caulixtla.com/JP-08/).

When the JP-08 came out, an analog polyphonic was very expensive.
Yes, synthesizers were quite affordable; I was able to get a very
flexible Juno-Di was under $600, but it was somewhat thin and digital
sounding.  Analog monophonics were around at reasonable prices
(Minibrute, etc.) but analog polyphony was still quite expensive.

The JP-08 gave us something which sounded really nice and analog (and,
at the time, there were huge debates about whether ACB sounded like
analog) and reasonable, if not great, polyphony for under $500. It made
it possible for me to give [the album I was working on at the
time](https://caulixtla.bandcamp.com/album/ocean-path)
a very much analog sound, and it really improved the sound of the albums
I released that decade. Even [my most recent album from last
year](https://caulixtla.bandcamp.com/album/trapped-in-the-woodland-faerie-ring)
has one song which is 100% JP-08 (with external effects).

Soon after, the Korg Minilogue made real analog polyphony affordable,
and Behringer later on made polyphonic analog no longer a Velben
good.

The JP-08 filled a niche, low cost (almost) analog polyphony, which was
not being filled by anyone else at the time, so it made a lot of sense
to get one.

The JP-08’s spiritual successor, the just-released Jupiter-08, is for
a very different market.  As I already mentioned, real analog polyphony
is a lot more cost effective than it was.  In addition, the Jupiter-08
uses the same sound engine (ABM) as their Zen Core synthesizers, which
has more polyphony but sounds less analog than the JP-08’s ACB did.
The JP-08 tried to sound as much like a classic Jupiter-8 as possible
using digital circuitry; the Jupiter-8 emphasizes polyphony, effects,
and flexibility over being an accurate Jupiter-8 recreation.
