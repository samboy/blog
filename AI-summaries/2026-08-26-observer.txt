The text focuses on Nathan Cofnas, a Cambridge academic whose public views on race and DEI became controversial, and who later drew attention by accusing Jason Arday of plagiarism.

### Main points

Cofnas accused Jason Arday of plagiarism: Cofnas posted allegations on Substack that parts of Arday’s PhD had been plagiarised. He also criticized Arday’s Cambridge appointment as an example of “DEI ideology” and questioned Arday’s intellectual ability.

Cofnas has controversial views on race: He describes himself as a “race realist” and has argued that race is connected to intelligence. He has also claimed that without DEI, Black academics would largely disappear from elite positions outside sports and entertainment, and has called for opposition to DEI initiatives.

His views led to protests and complaints: More than 1,200 Cambridge students and academics signed a petition calling for his dismissal, while 58 students formally complained about him. Some accused him of promoting ideas associated with eugenics and discriminatory rhetoric.

Emmanuel College dismissed him: In April 2024, the college said his views on race and DEI challenged its “core values and mission.” Cofnas subsequently sued the college for belief discrimination, with support from the Free Speech Union, but lost the case.

Cambridge later dismissed student complaints: Despite the controversy, Cambridge dismissed the 58 complaints in late 2025. The university's updated free-speech rules protected controversial or unpopular opinions as long as they remained within the law.

Cofnas now works in Belgium: He is currently at Ghent University and continues to publish his views on race on X and Substack.

Critics question his motivation in the Arday case: Black studies academic Kehinde Andrews argues that Cofnas's accusations resemble a broader political strategy of targeting prominent Black academics. Andrews suggests that the plagiarism allegations received disproportionate attention because of underlying hostility toward Black academics.

### Overall takeaway

The article presents two intertwined controversies: allegations of plagiarism against Jason Arday and the background of Nathan Cofnas, the person who publicized them. It emphasizes that Cofnas is himself a highly controversial figure whose views on race and DEI led to protests, complaints, and his dismissal from Emmanuel College, while also noting that Cambridge's free-speech rules ultimately protected his expression.
