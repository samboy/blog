The text reports that Nathan Cofnas, a postdoctoral researcher at Ghent University, has been suspended while the university investigates his conduct after he publicly accused the late Cambridge professor Jason Arday of plagiarism and questioned his claimed achievements.

### Main points

Cofnas accused Arday of plagiarism, claiming plagiarism-detection software found numerous passages in Arday's work that were copied with minimal changes.

The allegations sparked weeks of public and media controversy before Arday died on 14 August, aged 41. Arday denied the plagiarism allegations.

Ghent University suspended Cofnas as a precaution while conducting a preliminary disciplinary investigation into allegations that he discriminated against Arday.

The university emphasized its commitment to academic freedom and open debate, but said those freedoms have limits when they infringe on others' rights or involve discrimination, hatred, or racism.

Cofnas has attracted controversy previously over his views on race and intelligence. His role at a Cambridge college ended in 2024 following backlash over comments about Black people and meritocracy.

The controversy also involved questions about Arday's academic record and extraordinary charitable endurance claims, including claims involving marathons and long-distance treadmill running.

Arday had become Cambridge's youngest Black professor in 2023. He had resigned from his sociology of education professorship shortly before his death.

Ghent's leadership said Arday's death should lead to reflection on how academics and others are treated when they become the focus of public controversy.

### Bottom line

The story centers on the fallout from Cofnas's public scrutiny of Arday. Ghent University has now taken disciplinary action against Cofnas, while stressing that legitimate academic criticism is protected but must be balanced against responsibilities concerning dignity and discrimination.
