In a Bridgeport courtroom, Superior Court Judge Tracy Lee Drayton rejected Nikki Yovino’s bid to shorten the remaining half of her three-year probation term.

The  Long Island woman, now living with relatives in Ridgefield and hoping to return to South Setauket, did not appear at the hearing. Her attorney, Ryan O’Neill, offered no comment afterward. A prosecutor opposed the reduction, pointing out that Yovino had failed to provide evidence of completing the required mental-health evaluation and treatment that formed part of her probation conditions.

Yovino’s legal troubles stem from an October 2016 report she made to police. Then 19 and a student at Sacred Heart University, she claimed two football players had held her down and forced her to have sex in a basement bathroom during a party in Bridgeport. Both men acknowledged a sexual encounter but insisted it was consensual; they later left the university.

Three months after her initial statement, during an interview at her Long Island home, Yovino admitted she had invented the allegation. According to the arrest warrant, she said the story was “the first thing that came to mind” because she feared losing another male student as a friend and potential boyfriend and hoped the accusation would make him angry and sympathetic toward her.

In August 2018 she pleaded guilty to two counts of second-degree falsely reporting an incident and one count of interfering with police. She received a three-year prison sentence with the final two years suspended, ultimately serving one year behind bars followed by 16 months of probation before seeking the reduction that was denied.

The two former players have filed a civil suit against her, arguing the false complaint forced them out of Sacred Heart. At her sentencing, one of them, Malik St. Hilaire, told the court he had “no way” to clear his name and that his life would never be the same.
