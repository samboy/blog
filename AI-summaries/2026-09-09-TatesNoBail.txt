A federal judge in Miami denied Andrew and Tristan Tate bail while they fight extradition to the U.K. The brothers face 59 criminal charges, including rape and human trafficking for sexual exploitation, which they deny.

### Main points

Bail denied: Judge Lauren F. Louis ruled that the brothers did not show the “special circumstances” required for bail in an international extradition case.

Reason for denial: Prosecutors argued they are both a flight risk and a danger to the community, citing their wealth, multiple passports, private aircraft/boats, and statements they have made online.

Defense's argument: The Tates said their online claims are exaggerated personas designed for their audience. They offered to surrender their passports, wear GPS monitors, and undergo home inspections.

U.K. charges: The brothers are wanted in the U.K. on 59 charges dating from 2010–2017, including rape and human trafficking. They maintain they are innocent.

Romania connection: They previously faced similar allegations in Romania. After Romania lifted their travel restrictions in February 2025, they moved to Florida. Romania has since filed a new indictment alleging additional offenses, including sex with minors and money laundering.

U.S. political angle: The brothers have supporters among some U.S. right-wing figures and have expressed support for Donald Trump, but the White House has said it will not intervene in the extradition case.

What happens next: The U.K. must submit its formal extradition request by September 16. A Miami federal court will then determine whether the legal requirements for extradition—including probable cause—are satisfied.

Potentially lengthy process: If extradition is certified, the Tates can seek judicial review, request a stay, and potentially appeal to the Supreme Court. The process could take years rather than months.

Final decision: After the court process and appeals, the matter would ultimately go to the U.S. State Department, where the brothers could ask the secretary of state to block extradition.

### Bottom line

The Tates remain detained in Miami, and the immediate issue is not whether they are guilty, but whether they should be extradited to the U.K. to face the criminal charges there.
