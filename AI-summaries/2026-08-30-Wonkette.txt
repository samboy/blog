The text criticizes a New York Post article that publicly identified an anonymous paramedic who used OnlyFans to earn extra income during the pandemic. The author argues that the Post deliberately exposed and shamed her rather than reporting her story responsibly.

### Main points

The paramedic asked to remain anonymous, but the Post published identifying information anyway.

Reporters contacted her employer and mother, putting her job and personal life at risk.

The article emphasized explicit details from her OnlyFans content, which the author argues was unnecessary unless the goal was to create a scandal.

The Post also quoted another paramedic who criticized her for earning money through sex work rather than taking additional shifts.

The paramedic's employer had policies concerning "inappropriate conduct" on and off duty, creating a real possibility that she could lose her job.  Following the article, she deleted her OnlyFans content and restricted her social-media accounts because she feared professional consequences.  A friend created a GoFundMe to help her financially.

The woman defended her decision, explaining that she needed additional income, wanted financial independence, and had never mixed her OnlyFans work with her job as a paramedic.

She said she had warned the reporter that publishing her identity could threaten her safety and employment, but felt that her concerns were ignored.

The author strongly condemns the Post's coverage, portraying it as sex-shaming and misogynistic, and compares its tone to online "incel" fantasies about punishing women for their sexuality.

Overall, the text argues that selling sexual content privately should not make someone a less competent paramedic, and that the greater ethical problem was the newspaper's decision to expose someone who had explicitly requested privacy.

### Central argument

The author believes the Post turned an ordinary story about a working woman trying to make ends meet into a public shaming campaign, potentially putting her livelihood and safety at risk for no legitimate journalistic reason.
