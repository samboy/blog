A tragic incident in Frankfort, Illinois, unfolded on a Tuesday afternoon when a neighbor discovered a lifeless 2-year-old boy in a basement and intervened. The child, Barrett Walsh, was found hanging from a rafter with a ligature around his neck and his feet off the ground. The neighbor removed the ligature, lowered him, performed CPR, and called emergency services. Paramedics found the toddler in cardiac arrest, transported him to a hospital in New Lenox, and he was pronounced dead around 5 p.m. Preliminary findings pointed to death by asphyxia from ligature compression of the neck.

At the same residence, officers and the neighbor encountered the boy’s mother, 40-year-old Corie Walsh, in an upstairs bathroom. She was in a bloody tub with self-inflicted cuts on her wrists and thighs, and a neighbor had already taken a knife from her hand. Walsh allegedly told responding officers that she had harmed her son because he was the “devil” and the “anti-Christ.” She also asked why the knife had been taken from her while she was attempting to end her own life. A baby in the home was unharmed, as were two older children who returned around the time of the discovery. Walsh’s husband, who had been out of state, flew home that evening.

Earlier that same day, more than 1,000 miles from the Massachusetts courtroom where a jury was deliberating in the high-profile Lindsay Clancy murder trial, Walsh had been texting friends about the case until roughly 12:30 p.m. Prosecutors later said she had become “very invested” in the proceedings involving the former nurse accused of killing her three children.

Walsh was charged with first-degree murder. Her attorney described the events as the result of a mental-health crisis and a psychotic episode, calling the situation a tragedy for the entire family. Prosecutors sought to keep her detained pending a hearing, citing alleged statements about harming her other children and her husband. The Illinois Department of Child and Family Services reported no prior contact with the family. A GoFundMe campaign was started to help support the surviving children and their father through the immediate aftermath.
