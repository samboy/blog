I learned sexual morality from Marshall. This is a continuation of my series on Marshall, who I discuss in two previous blog entries.

Sexual values

Marshall and I had conversations about sex and relationships. I was still young and a virgin, so there was a lot I still didn’t know about relationships and sex. While sitting with Marshall in his office, we had a conversation about sex that started like this:

I said, “What sexual values should someone have, Marshall?”

Marshall said, “Sam, I will tell you the truth. I haven’t been perfect about my sexuality. I have made a lot of mistakes and have done things I regret doing.”

I said, “Why do you regret having sex? Sex feels good.”

Marshall said, “Yes, you’re right, Sam. Sex does feel good—while you’re getting a piece of ass and having sex with the girl. It’s what you feel afterwards that sucks.

See, when you have sex with someone who don’t really know and don’t love, only the physical act of sex feels good. After the sex is done, you feel shallow and empty.” I said, “So, Marshall, it isn’t a good idea to have casual sex?”

Marshall said, “No, Sam, it isn’t. The truth is that masturbation is more satisfying that having a meaningless purely physical relationship with a girl. Having sex without having feelings for the girl is not what you want to do.”

I said, “What kind of sexual relationship is the best?”

Marshall said, “Again, Sam, I haven’t met this ideal, but the best sex you have is to get together with someone you truly love, have a monogamous relationship, and only have sex with one person for your entire life. That is the best sex you can have.”

I took those words to heart. I lost my virginity to my first wife; we had a baby together and I was there next to her bed as she succumbed to cancer. The sex with her was fantastic and never got old; the more I knew her, the better the sex got.

Another MaraDNS bugfix

September 1, 2026

Another patch for a very minor security bug in MaraDNS.

Bug description

Deadwood, when sending a RFC8482 reply to an ANY query, would transmit over the network 19 bytes of uninitialized memory. This is a possible (but unlikely) leak of private data. This bug was fixed on September 1, 2026, in commit cd87cfd9; a patch is available, and the Windows 32-bit binary was fixed in commit 0f46565c.

An analysis of the packets for an unpatched Deadwood in Ubuntu 26.04 show no significant information is leaked in those 19 bytes.

This bug does not merit a new MaraDNS release.

Thoughts on AI

Since this is yet another AI-assisted security report, let me discuss my feelings about AI. I am not a big fan of AI:

  • Environmental impact of countless datacenters being built

  • Sky high memory and storage prices, making computers too expensive

  • Jobs being replaced by AI, causing high unemployment

  • People using LLMs as therapists or even as girl/​boyfriends

  • Countless security reports to trawl through, most of which are minor

I am proud, however, that no major security hole has been found with MaraDNS in the age of AI (knock on wood). It’s things like:

  • Denial of service of just the DNS-over-TCP part of Deadwood (which is disabled by default)

  • A very hard to exploit buffer overflow in a program which hasn’t even compiled since 2022

  • 19 bytes of unallocated heap data can be transmitted over the network. To get any important information from this is nay to impossible.

How I use AI

AI isn’t entirely bad: I would rather have AI answer a question for me than try and find an answer on Stack Overflow.

Also, it’s nice to be able to generate AI summaries of articles, so I can have a local mirror of articles I link to in my blog. Indeed, the AI summaries solve a problem AI has created: Since countless web bots are now being run to train LLMs, websites have retaliated by blocking a large number of IPs. To wit, I need to use a VPN to access one of the big archiving websites out there.

That in mind, I can’t count on a web archive making some news article I link to in my blog permanent anymore; instead I now make an AI summary (to remove copyright from the article; note that this legal trick only works when the article is reporting on facts, since facts are not protected by copyright) so people can get an overview of the news I comment on in my blog.

Why I stick with Lua 5.1

Sometimes, people argue that Lua 5.1 should no longer be supported, and everyone should move on to Lua 5.5. This is not feasible.

As a poster on Lobsters points out, PUC Lua breaks compatibility between point releases, and applications that embed Lua need to not break scripts used by the applications.

There’s a reason I forked Lua 5.1 and named it Lunacy—that guarantees that any future changes to Lua don’t break the scripts I use to make this blog nor the scripts that make MaraDNS’ documentation.
