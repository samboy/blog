Years after a party at Sacred Heart University, the two football players Nikki Yovino once accused of rape are now pursuing a defamation lawsuit against her.

In the interim, Yovino—who declined to appear or speak in a recent documentary examining her case—has kept a low profile. Filmmaker de Leon reports occasional contact and describes her as “doing okay” after enduring significant hardship, including a one-year prison sentence that followed her guilty plea. She chose not to participate in the film, telling the storytellers they could recount events without her active involvement—a decision de Leon fully respects.

The original police investigation took an unusual path. Rather than questioning the accused athletes, authorities focused on Yovino herself. Bridgeport Police Detective Walberto Cotto Jr. later acknowledged in an interview with de Leon that he employed a deliberate “ruse” (also known as the Reid technique) during questioning, lying to Yovino in an effort to extract a confession. He further conceded he had been unaware that one of the players she named had faced a separate sexual-assault allegation only a month earlier.

The documentary discusses these details at length even without Yovino’s on-camera contribution, highlighting how the case unfolded from her initial report of an assault in a party bathroom to the charges of filing a false police report that ultimately led to her conviction and incarceration.
