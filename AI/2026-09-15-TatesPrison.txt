The Federal Detention Centre in Miami holds Andrew and Tristan Tate in its special housing unit as they await a formal UK extradition request over rape and human trafficking allegations, which they deny. Arrested in mid-July on provisional warrants, the brothers face no U.S. charges yet remain confined while British authorities finalize paperwork.

Broader reports describe the facility as overcrowded and unsanitary, housing both pretrial inmates and ICE detainees. Families and detainees have alleged persistent smells of waste, cockroaches in living areas and food, broken toilets, and extreme heat without air conditioning. A Human Rights Watch account detailed new arrivals being told “welcome to hell,” held for hours without food or water, subjected to verbal abuse, invasive searches, and forced to eat with hands cuffed behind their backs. Two ICE detainees have died there, with one case under FBI review.

The Tates, kept apart from the general population for institutional safety, have voiced their own grievances through lawyers and a handwritten note: limited recreation (five hours weekly), one monthly phone call, lack of grooming items, weight loss claims (disputed by a judge who found no decline and possible slight gains), a diet heavy on ramen and chips, and residual pepper spray drifting into their cells without decontamination. Prison officials maintain the conditions are constitutional and within discretion, emphasizing security reviews and the duty to protect both inmates and staff.
