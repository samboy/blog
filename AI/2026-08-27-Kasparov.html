Political attacks on Fauci have made genuine accountability harder. The author views Republican efforts to punish Fauci as excessively partisan and argues that framing the choice as either criminal prosecution or complete exoneration prevents a nuanced examination of his mistakes.

Not every mistake is a crime. The author distinguishes between people whose conduct may warrant criminal prosecution and figures such as AOC and Fauci, whose errors should instead be examined through public scrutiny, hearings, and honest self-reflection.

The central thesis

Moderation and accountability are preferable to punishment or absolution. The conclusion argues that democracy benefits from a middle ground: acknowledging wrongdoing, investigating it honestly, and learning from it without resorting to either cancellation or political vengeance.
