The text is an investigative account of Nikita Tyukalo, a young Seattle-area entrepreneur who allegedly built a large-scale webcam and OnlyFans operation that prosecutors say evolved into a sex-trafficking and organized-crime enterprise. The unusual twist is that some of the men who paid to watch the women—self-described “simps”—became amateur investigators who helped law enforcement build the case against him.

### Main points

The operation began as OnlyFans management.
Tyukalo founded Luxe Allure and later Nova Talent Management, recruiting young women with promises of easy money from online modeling. He and his employees allegedly took control of their accounts, passwords, finances, and earnings.

Tyukalo allegedly used coercion and violence.
Former models described physical assaults, threats, manipulation, withheld wages, restrictive contracts, intimidation, and pressure to perform increasingly explicit webcam content. One former girlfriend, identified as Gabby, said he beat and strangled her repeatedly and threatened to kill her if she spoke out.

The models were housed together and heavily controlled.
Several young women lived in luxury houses and apartments around Bellevue and were expected to stream on Chaturbate for roughly eight or more hours a day. Coaches allegedly dictated what they wore and did, discouraged breaks, and punished them for poor performance.

Money was allegedly taken from the women.
Models said they were promised hourly wages but frequently weren't paid. Some contracts allegedly gave Nova extensive claims over their earnings. In one account, a model received a $32,000 payment from a fan, after which her phone was taken and her bank account was allegedly drained.

The operation allegedly involved very young recruits.
Former associates said Tyukalo specifically sought women who looked young, and one woman said she was recruited while only 16. Recruiters allegedly disguised the sexual nature of the work by presenting it as ordinary modeling.

Andrew Tate's influence is a major theme.
Tyukalo openly admired Andrew Tate and reportedly modeled his business practices and attitudes toward women on Tate's teachings about webcam businesses and relationships.

The “simps” became investigators.
Viewers on the private forum SimpCity began noticing bruises, unusual behavior, men controlling the women off-camera, and indications that the models weren't acting freely. A group calling itself the White Knights gathered police records, contracts, videos, identifying information, and testimony from former models and their families.

Their investigation eventually reached police.
The White Knights contacted Bellevue police detective Alex Slusser in late 2025 and shared their findings. They continued monitoring the streams and reported incidents that they believed showed violence or coercion.

There was a disturbing January 2026 incident.
Viewers heard screaming during a livestream and saw Zara apparently distressed afterward. The simps alerted investigators, adding to concerns about what was happening inside the houses.

Tyukalo openly taunted his online critics.
He entered the investigators' Telegram chat, mocked them, bragged about his wealth and influence, and made increasingly extreme statements about violence and enslaving women. His final appearance allegedly included threats to beat and choke one of the women.

Authorities eventually arrested him.
In June 2026, a SWAT team raided his Bellevue mansion and arrested Tyukalo. Prosecutors charged him with four counts of human trafficking, money laundering, and leading an organized-crime group. Cory Connor and Kamal Muhammad were named as unindicted co-conspirators. Tyukalo pleaded not guilty.

The case exposes a broader ecosystem.
The article argues that Tyukalo wasn't an isolated phenomenon. Online “OFM” agencies have become a lucrative Gen-Z business model, and the article suggests that Tate-style content can normalize the exploitation of young women and encourage teenage boys to enter the industry.

The “heroes” are morally complicated.
The White Knights helped expose the alleged abuse, but they were simultaneously consumers of the very sexualized content that generated the business. Some continued ranking and objectifying the women even while claiming to protect them. The article deliberately highlights this contradiction.

### Central takeaway

The story is ultimately less about a single abusive webcam operator than about an ecosystem of exploitation: social-media influencers popularize a business model, young men turn it into an enterprise, vulnerable young women become the source of revenue, and even the consumers who recognize the abuse can remain entangled in it.

The most striking irony is that the same audience financially supporting the webcam economy became instrumental in exposing one of its alleged worst abuses—while remaining part of the demand that sustains the industry.
