Ulrika Jonsson has joined the chorus of criticism directed at Leonardo
DiCaprio’s long-standing preference for much younger partners. In
her newspaper column the 55-year-old presenter described the idea of a
nearly 50-year-old man pairing with a teenager as not merely distasteful
but “sickening,” adding that the thought of herself dating an 18- or
19-year-old “gives me the creeps.”

The latest spark was a photograph of DiCaprio, 48, with 19-year-old
model Eden Polani at a Los Angeles party. Although both sides insist
there is no romance—sources say they were simply seated next to each
other—the image revived long-running unease about the actor’s dating
pattern. Polani was not yet born when Titanic was released and had her
high-school years disrupted by the pandemic, details that amplified
online outrage.

Jonsson framed her objection through her own experience, asking why anyone
would choose a partner so young that the age gap feels more parental than
romantic. She is not alone; comedians and singers including Katherine Ryan
and Dionne Warwick have voiced similar disapproval. DiCaprio’s history
includes a series of relationships that ended around the woman’s 25th
birthday, most recently with Camila Morrone. His current reported partner,
Victoria Lamas, is 23, while a brief public appearance with Gigi Hadid
marked a rare departure from his usual age range.

The episode underscores a broader cultural discomfort with large age
disparities when the younger person is still in their late teens,
a discomfort Jonsson articulated in unusually blunt terms.
