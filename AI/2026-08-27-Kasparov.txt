The author argues that the political and cultural climate of the early 2020s—described as “Woke 1”—became excessively punitive and intolerant of dissent. They agree with Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez’s recent characterization of that period as “crazy,” while arguing that the appropriate response is accountability rather than cancellation or political retaliation.

### Main points

“Woke 1” went too far.
The author criticizes practices such as land acknowledgements, firings, social ostracism, and aggressive policing of speech, arguing that they became socially destructive.

The author connects political disorder to backlash.
They argue that some progressives excused rioting and disorder during the 2020 racial-justice protests, which helped create public fear of chaos and, in the author’s view, contributed to support for more authoritarian responses from the political right.

Accountability was missing.
Instead of determining whether someone actually did something wrong and allowing for rehabilitation, the culture often emphasized punishment and cancellation.

AOC should examine her past views rather than simply disown them.
The author does not advocate canceling or prosecuting Ocasio-Cortez. Instead, they want her to seriously examine why some of her earlier ideas were wrong.

The same principle applies to Anthony Fauci and COVID-19.
The author strongly rejects vaccine denial and pseudoscience but argues that Fauci nevertheless made serious mistakes, particularly by projecting certainty about issues—including the pandemic's origins—when his private views were more uncertain.

COVID-era debate became too hostile to dissent.
The author argues that people who questioned prevailing explanations of the pandemic, including the possibility of a lab origin, faced professional and reputational consequences that discouraged legitimate scientific inquiry.

Political attacks on Fauci have made genuine accountability harder.
The author views Republican efforts to punish Fauci as excessively partisan and argues that framing the choice as either criminal prosecution or complete exoneration prevents a nuanced examination of his mistakes.

Not every mistake is a crime.
The author distinguishes between people whose conduct may warrant criminal prosecution and figures such as AOC and Fauci, whose errors should instead be examined through public scrutiny, hearings, and honest self-reflection.

### The central thesis

Moderation and accountability are preferable to punishment or absolution.
The conclusion argues that democracy benefits from a middle ground: acknowledging wrongdoing, investigating it honestly, and learning from it without resorting to either cancellation or political vengeance.

### In one sentence

The piece argues that the failures of both “Woke 1” and the COVID-era establishment should be confronted through honest, proportionate accountability—not cancellation, censorship, or politically motivated punishment.
