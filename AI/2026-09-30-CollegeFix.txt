In a Connecticut courtroom, a 20-year-old former Sacred Heart University student named Nikki Yovino prepares to face a jury on charges that could send her to prison for up to six years.

Jury selection is set to begin June 4, with the trial itself scheduled to open June 18 under two female prosecutors. The case centers on her report of being raped by two football players in a bathroom during a party, followed months later by her admission that the claim was invented.

According to the arrest warrant, Yovino told investigators she fabricated the sexual-assault allegation because it was the first story that occurred to her and she hoped it would make another male student angry and sympathetic, preserving him as a friend and potential boyfriend. Police noted inconsistencies in her initial account that prompted a second interview, during which she abandoned the claim of nonconsent. The accused players maintained the encounter was consensual, and a witness reported overhearing Yovino express interest in having sex with them.

Sacred Heart University had already taken action against the players before any formal hearing: they were removed from the team and had scholarships revoked, after which both withdrew from the school. University officials had earlier stated the players were not expelled or stripped of aid solely because of the allegations, yet contemporaneous reporting and statements from the detective and other sources indicated scholarship losses and eligibility impacts.

The episode is not isolated at the Catholic university. Only a month before Yovino’s arrest, Sacred Heart publicly apologized to a local man after wrongly circulating his name and photo on leaflets following a different reported off-campus rape; the man had filed a $1 million defamation suit whose other settlement terms remain private.

Yovino’s attorneys have maintained that she stands by her original account of being pushed into a bathroom and assaulted. The case has drawn comparisons to high-profile false-report incidents, with critics noting that “trauma-informed” approaches used on some campuses and by some police departments might never have uncovered the alleged fabrication.
