The Trump administration has paused all U.S. immigrant visa applications while the State Department trains consular officers on stricter screening rules. The stated goal is to prevent immigrants from becoming dependent on U.S. public benefits, but the move is part of a broader effort to make legal immigration more restrictive.

### Main points

All immigrant visa applications are currently paused. Consulates and embassies began adjusting appointments in early August to allow for additional training.

Existing appointments have been canceled or postponed, potentially forcing applicants who spent significant money and made major life arrangements to start waiting again. No clear rescheduling timeline has been given.

The new screening focuses partly on whether applicants might rely on U.S. public assistance, reviving the administration's broader “public charge” approach.

The pause comes alongside other immigration restrictions, including increased scrutiny of applicants' social media histories and higher visa-processing costs.

Plans to revoke visas for some asylum seekers who originally entered the U.S. on tourist or business visas, potentially affecting up to 200,000 people.

The administration previously tried to restrict visa applications from 75 countries on similar public-charge grounds, but a federal judge overturned that policy.

Courts have also blocked other Trump immigration measures, including an attempt to suspend the U.S. refugee system entirely.

Immigration advocates and legal experts argue that the latest restrictions create unnecessary barriers for people who have followed the legal immigration process.

### Bottom line

The pause appears to be another major step toward a more selective and restrictive U.S. immigration system, with immediate uncertainty for people already waiting for immigrant visas.
